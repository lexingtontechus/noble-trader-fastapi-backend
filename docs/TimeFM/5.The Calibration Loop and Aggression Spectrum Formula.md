The **Calibration Loop** and its associated **CalibrationRecord schema** represent the system's "Self-Correction" layer. This process is designed to ensure that the mathematical probabilities generated by the EV Engine ($P\_{win}$) align with the actual frequency of wins in live or paper markets 1, 2\.

### 1\. The Calibration Loop: What and Where

The CalibrationRecord is a database model (typically stored in **Supabase/PostgreSQL**) used to audit the performance of the trading intelligence pipeline 3, 4\. It serves as a historical ledger that pairs a specific **signal** (at the moment of entry) with its eventual **outcome** 1, 2\.

* **Tracking per Symbol:** The system logs these records for each symbol to identify asset-specific biases 1\. For instance, a model might be "overconfident" in Bitcoin but "underconfident" in Forex pairs 2\.  
* **Key Fields Tracked:**  
* predictedPWin: The blended probability generated by the system (e.g., 0.65) 1\.  
* outcome: Whether the trade was a 'win' or a 'loss' 1\.  
* aggression: The strategy used (Passive, Mid, or Aggressive) to see which execution style is actually profitable 2\.  
* errorSource: Identifies if a loss was caused by a regime shift mid-trade or a signal flip 2\.

### 2\. Dynamic Adjustment of Signal Weights

The system uses a **Composite $P\_{win}$** formula:$$P\_{win} \= (w\_r \\times P\_{regime}) \+ (w\_i \\times P\_{imbalance}) \+ (w\_m \\times P\_{markov})$$The weights ($w\_r, w\_i, w\_m$) are currently configured at **40%, 30%, and 30%** respectively 5\. This process becomes dynamic through the Calibration Loop:

1. **Bias Detection:** A SQL query calculates the calibrationBias by subtracting the actual win rate from the avg\_predicted win rate 1\.  
2. **Weight Optimization:** If the CalibrationRecord shows that $P\_{markov}$ is consistently more accurate than $P\_{regime}$ for a specific asset (like SOL), the system can be programmed to increase $w\_m$ and decrease $w\_r$ for that symbol's configuration in Redis 5, 204, Conversation History.  
3. **Status Labeling:** The loop labels the daily journal as **"CALIBRATED," "OVERCONFIDENT," or "UNDERCONFIDENT"** 2\. If a system stays "Overconfident" for several days, it triggers a **Heavy Sweep** to re-optimize the underlying HMM parameters 2, 6\.

### 3\. The Aggression Spectrum Formula

The **Aggression Spectrum** is the bridge between mathematical expectation and exchange execution. It determines how "hard" the bot should fight for a fill based on the **Normalized EV per dollar risked ($EV\_{norm}$)** 7, 8\.  
**The Spectrum Tiers:**| EV Range | Strategy | Execution Logic | Target || :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- || **EV \> 0.40** | **Passive Limit** | Deep entry (offset from mid) | Capture **Maker Rebate** 7, 9 || **0.15 \< EV ≤ 0.40** | **Mid-Point Limit** | Standard entry at mid-price | Balanced fill probability 7, 9 || **EV ≤ 0.15** | **Aggressive/Market** | Immediate entry | **Guaranteed fill** before edge fades 7, 9 |  
**The Formula for $EV\_{norm}$:**The system first calculates raw Expected Value ($EV\_{raw}$):$$EV\_{raw} \= (P\_{win} \\times \\text{Profit}) \- (P\_{loss} \\times \\text{Stake})$$*Profit and Stake are measured in brick\_size × brick\_count units 10, 11\.*  
It then normalizes this value per dollar risked to make the aggression spectrum asset-agnostic:$$EV\_{norm} \= \\frac{EV\_{raw}}{\\text{Stake}}$$A **high $EV\_{norm}$** (e.g., 0.50) indicates a massive mathematical edge, allowing the bot to place a **Passive Limit** order and wait for a better price, as it can afford the risk of the trade not filling. A **low $EV\_{norm}$** (e.g., 0.10) indicates a fleeting edge that must be seized immediately with a **Market/Aggressive** order 7, 8\.
