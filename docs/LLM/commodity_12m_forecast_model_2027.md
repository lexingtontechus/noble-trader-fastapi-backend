## Cross-Commodity Correlation Notes

1. **Hormuz is the master variable** — Brent, all refined products, EU gas, DAP, and sulfur are all directly exposed to the Strait reopening timeline. A deal around November midterms (ING base case) would trigger synchronized mean-reversion in energy, diesel cracks, and gas.

2. **El Niño is the agricultural master variable** — Rice (most exposed), palm oil, sugar, and corn all face production risk. If the super El Niño materializes (63% chance), agricultural prices re-rate sharply higher across the board.

3. **Diesel ↔ Jet divergence** — Diesel is structurally tighter than jet. The regrade spread is the cleanest expression of this: short jet / long diesel. Asia has export flexibility; Europe and the US don't.

4. **Sugar ↔ Ethanol** — Brazil's sugar/ethanol mix is the swing variable for global sugar supply. Higher crude oil prices incentivize ethanol → less sugar → higher sugar prices. This creates an oil-to-sugar transmission channel.

5. **Cotton ↔ Corn** — Competing crop for acreage. Cotton returns slightly above corn currently, supporting cotton acreage but capping corn upside. If cotton continues its bullish run, corn acreage may shift.

6. **EU Gas ↔ Diesel** — European gasoil prices and TTF are correlated through power generation switching and industrial demand. A cold winter lifts both simultaneously.
